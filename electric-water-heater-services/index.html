Most electric tank water heaters use two heating elements and two thermostats, and they take turns rather than running together. The upper thermostat has priority: it heats the top of the tank first, then hands power down to the lower element, which does most of the day-to-day work. A high-limit switch (the red reset button above the upper thermostat) cuts power if the water ever overheats. That arrangement makes symptoms surprisingly useful. No hot water at all usually points to the upper element, the upper thermostat, a tripped high-limit, or the breaker. Hot water that runs out quickly usually means the lower element or lower thermostat has quit. In Norfolk, VA, we see all of these every week.

Norfolk Water Heater tests electric heaters with a meter, not a hunch. With power confirmed off, our technicians measure element resistance and look for a short to the tank, a common failure that can keep tripping the high-limit. Then, following careful live-testing procedure, we verify voltage at the thermostats and watch how power moves from the upper side to the lower side. We look for scorched wires, melted insulation, and loose terminal screws, since a loose connection can heat up enough to damage the thermostat and the wiring around it. If the reset button has tripped, we find out why before resetting it, because a high-limit that trips is doing its job.

Some electric heaters need a new element and a flush, and some need an honest conversation about replacement. We handle both. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Hot water that runs out after just a few minutes
  • The high-limit reset popping again after you push it
  • Crackling noises as the heater runs, often from sediment
  • Browned wiring or a burnt odor from behind the access cover
  • Water dripping from an element gasket or pooling under the tank
  • Cold water everywhere, even after waiting
  • A breaker that trips when the heater calls for heat
  • Water that comes out scalding hot

Why Hire a Professional for Electric Water Heater Services?

Two things make electric water heater repair trickier than it looks: the power involved and the overlap between symptoms. These heaters run on 240-volt circuits, and the thermostats and elements are wired to terminals that stay live unless the breaker is off and verified. On the diagnostic side, a heater that will not heat might have a failed element, a bad thermostat, a tripped reset, or a wiring problem, and a reset that keeps tripping is a warning, not an annoyance. Guessing gets expensive fast.

Professional service gets you the right fix the first time and a safe heater afterward. Rather than guess, we meter each element and thermostat, check for shorts to the tank, and inspect every wire and terminal for heat damage. Repairs are done with the tank drained and refilled correctly, and we verify amp draw once power is back on. When replacement makes more sense, we confirm the breaker size and wire gauge match the new heater and install the expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps your code requires. The result is dependable hot water without surprises.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • If the reset button trips, have it checked rather than pressing it over and over
  • In hard water areas, a softener or low-watt-density elements can extend element life
  • Lift the T&P valve lever yearly to make sure it opens and reseats
  • Never turn the breaker back on until the tank is completely full of water
  • Keep the thermostats set around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Flush sediment from the tank once or twice a year so it cannot bury the lower element
  • Have the sacrificial anode inspected every three to five years
