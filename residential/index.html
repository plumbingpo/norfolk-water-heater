Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Norfolk Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, handling everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly corrodes away, and the tank lining starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection right away. An early diagnosis usually keeps a repairable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one decides for you. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. Once a year, we drain and flush the tank, check the anode rod, test the relief valve, look over the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all matched to the equipment in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve is ready to vent pressure if something goes wrong. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Sized for Your Household

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Norfolk Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the flow rate in GPM to the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up after two teenagers start showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver continuous hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line sizing, electrical service, and the footprint, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Norfolk

Homes around Norfolk run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Norfolk Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before the damage spreads. We also verify the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
