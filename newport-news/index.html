How Newport News Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Water heaters in and around Newport News deal with a familiar set of problems. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod is often used up, leaving the steel open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Code is another piece. Swapping out an older heater often means bringing the install up to current code: a thermal expansion tank if the water system is closed, a relief valve drain line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Homes on well water have their own issues, such as heavier sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that sometimes develops when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Call (757) 290-7391 and we'll take a look.
