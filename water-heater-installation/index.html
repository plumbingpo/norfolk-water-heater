Every installation starts with sizing and location. Location is the next question. Gas units need a way to vent outdoors and enough air for combustion. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater

Comparing DIY and Professional Work

A few tasks, like checking the temperature setting or lifting the relief valve lever to test it, are fine for homeowners, but most water heater work involves gas lines, venting, 240-volt circuits, or a tank full of scalding water, and that belongs with a professional.
